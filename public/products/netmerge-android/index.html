NetMerge for Android

Wi-Fi and mobile data, at the same time.

An Android app that sends your phone's traffic over Wi-Fi and mobile data together, with more of it on whichever is faster.

Betav0.2.0-beta.4Android 10 and newer
Download the betaSource on GitHub

The problem

Your phone has two ways online and uses one.

When Wi-Fi is connected, Android leaves mobile data idle, even when the Wi-Fi is slow or about to drop.

NetMerge for Android runs a VPN on the phone itself that sees every app's traffic. Each new connection goes out over Wi-Fi or mobile data, and the faster network gets more of them. Your traffic is not sent through a server of mine.

What it can and cannot do

Apps that open many connections

  • Wi-Fibusy
  • Mobilebusy

Downloads, app stores, browsers and streaming open many connections. Each new one is sent over Wi-Fi or mobile data, with more going to the faster network.

One single connection

  • Wi-Fibusy
  • Mobileidle

A single connection stays on one network. NetMerge does not join two networks into one faster pipe, so it does not double your speed.

What it does

  • 01

    Learns each network

    It works out how fast Wi-Fi and mobile data are, and how quickly each answers, and shares new connections to match. A very slow mobile network gets almost nothing, so web pages are not held up by it.

  • 02

    Survives a dropout

    When a network leaves, its connections close at once and apps carry on over the other one. With Wi-Fi or mobile data switched off mid-download, the longest stall in my tests was half a second.

  • 03

    A built-in speed test

    Wi-Fi alone, mobile data alone, then both together, for download and upload. It tells you how much mobile data the test used.

  • 04

    Mobile data on your terms

    Use it only when it helps, set a daily limit, and keep merging off mobile data while roaming.

  • 05

    Ads and trackers blocked

    Optional block lists stop ads, trackers, malware and phishing sites for every app while NetMerge is on, with an allow list for exceptions.

  • 06

    A battery guard

    Merging pauses at a battery level you choose and starts again by itself once the phone has charged. Apps keep the normal network meanwhile.

  • 07

    Leave apps out

    For apps that do not work with a VPN.

  • 08

    Starts by itself

    After a restart, with Android's Always-on VPN setting. There is also a quick settings tile and a notification with the live speed.

  • 09

    Tells you about updates

    NetMerge says when a newer version is out. You can also check under Settings, About.

  • 10

    Your traffic is yours

    It goes straight out on your own networks, never through a server of mine. No account and no ads.

How it has been tested

  • 01

    On my own phone

    A Galaxy S26 Ultra on Android 17, with real Wi-Fi and real mobile data. Every feature was run, including a restart with Always-on VPN, 20 fast starts and stops, and a 3-hour session. All pass after the fixes.

  • 02

    On other makers' phones

    A Galaxy S22, a Pixel 8a, a Motorola razr plus 2024, an Oppo and a Xiaomi 14 (Android 13 to 16), plus two virtual phones, in Firebase Test Lab. All pass. Those phones have Wi-Fi only, so this checks that the app starts, the VPN comes up and pages open. It does not check merging.

  • 03

    On every change

    Android 14 and 15 emulators with Wi-Fi and mobile data run the full set of checks.

  • 04

    What the tests caught

    Every bug found so far is fixed. One left the phone with no internet after a restart, one stopped websites opening when mobile data was very slow, and with a large font the numbers on the speed dial overlapped.

How to install the beta

  1. On the phone, open the download page and pick the file ending in arm64-v8a.apk. It suits most phones. The one named universal works on any.
  2. When Android asks, allow your browser to install apps.
  3. If you installed a test build before, uninstall it first. The beta is signed with a different key.
  4. After that, each new beta installs over the last one and keeps your settings. NetMerge tells you when one is out.

What it asks of your phone

  • 01

    It runs as a VPN

    That is how one app can see and steer every other app's traffic. The VPN lives on the phone itself. Your traffic is not sent through a server of mine.

  • 02

    One VPN at a time

    Android allows a single VPN to be active, so NetMerge cannot run together with another VPN app.

  • 03

    Permissions

    Network access and network state, a notification while merging is on, and the VPN permission you approve on first start.

  • 04

    Diagnostics, on by default

    To find bugs on other people's phones, NetMerge sends a small report every 6 hours or so, on Wi-Fi only: its own event log, its settings, the phone's make, model and Android version, and a random number made at install. After a crash it also sends where it crashed. You can switch this off on the welcome screen or in Settings.

  • 05

    Never sent

    The sites or apps you use, addresses, your traffic, your name or account, or any phone identifier.

  • 06

    What it does not ask for

    No location, no contacts, no files, no account.

Honest limits

  • It is a beta, the first version meant for other people's phones. Expect rough edges.
  • It is not on Google Play yet. You install it from the download page.
  • Merging has been measured on one phone only. On the other phones, only starting, the VPN and opening pages were checked.
  • Not tested yet: roaming abroad on a real phone, a Wi-Fi network that needs a login page, overnight battery limits on Samsung phones, and phones belonging to anyone but me.
  • Merging uses mobile data, and keeping mobile data awake costs battery. How much it costs has not been measured properly yet.
  • If Android's Private DNS is set to a fixed provider, ad blocking cannot work.
  • Two downloads in the 3-hour session stalled, for 25 and 7 seconds. It has not happened again and I cannot explain it yet.
