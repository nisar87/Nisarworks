NetMerge

Every internet line you have, working at once.

Combines your cable, Wi-Fi and hotspot into one faster connection on Windows 11. No extra hardware, no paid service.

Releasedv3.17.5Windows 11 · Android in test builds
Download for WindowsSource on GitHub

The problem

Two connections at home. One doing all the work.

Plenty of homes have a cable line and a second way online: Wi-Fi from another provider, or a phone hotspot. Windows picks one and ignores the rest.

NetMerge hands every new connection your programs open to whichever line has the most room right now. Browsers, download managers and game launchers open many connections, so they end up drawing on all your lines together.

What it does

  • 01

    Three ways to merge

    Send each connection to the fastest line, keep each website on one line, or use one line with the others as backup.

  • 02

    A role for every line

    Use a line always, only when the others are busy, or only as a backup. Handy for a hotspot with limited data.

  • 03

    Calls stay steady

    Meeting apps are kept on one line, and new downloads move to the others while you are on a call.

  • 04

    Ads and trackers blocked

    Optional blocking with downloadable filter lists.

  • 05

    Dead lines step aside

    A line without internet is left out until it works again.

  • 06

    It tells you when merging cannot help

    If two lines leave through the same public address, NetMerge says so.

On the bench

NetMerge for Android

The same idea on a phone: Wi-Fi and mobile data at the same time. It runs as working test builds and is not released. On a Galaxy S26 Ultra with two fast networks, many-connection downloads measured about 38% faster, and dropping a network mid-download stalled it for under 3 seconds.

Honest limits

  • One single connection can only use one line. A single-stream speed test shows one line's speed. The gain comes from many connections at once.
  • Two lines from the same provider usually cannot add speed.
  • Other firewall or VPN drivers can conflict with NetMerge's virtual adapter. It warns you if it finds one.
  • The app is not code-signed yet, so Windows asks you to confirm the first run.
